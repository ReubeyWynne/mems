BEAR HUNT,
DEMYSTIFIED

How rally damage works in plain words — and the four things you should do about it.

30 min a run · every 48 hours · Forgehammer goldmine · F2P friendly

The one-liner

Remember one sentence and you basically know the whole event:

The lead's stats × your troops — with a square root on your count.

THE LEAD Sets the power

Everyone in the rally borrows the lead's stats. Same lead, same power, for all 15 of you. Your own attack stats don't matter.

YOU Make your damage

Each player's march is scored on its own, using the square root of its troop count. Your march. Your sqrt. Your damage.

That's it. Everything below is just what those two facts mean in practice.

The square root

The sqrt is the whole game. Here's the only number you need: double your march → +41%, not +100%.

Same troops. Just split across more marches:

1 march
1.0×
2 marches
1.4×
4 marches
2.0×
6 marches
2.4×
  • 2× the troops = +41%, not double
  • 4× the troops = 2.0×
  • 16× the troops = only 4.0×

First troops hit hardest. Every extra troop hits a little softer than the last. So bigger marches help a little — more marches help a lot.

The exact maths

Per player, per rally, damage is added over your three troop types:

Σ  baset × At(lead) × √(nt)

  • nt — troops of type t in your march.
  • At(lead) = (1 + atk%) × (1 + lethality%) of the lead, per type. Your own attack & lethality never enter it.
  • baset — the relative weights at T6: infantry ⅓, cavalry 1, archers 4.4/3 ≈ 1.47. The archer weight is (4/3) × 1.1 — the bear is all infantry and archers do +10% vs infantry. At T7+/TG3+ archers pick up another 1.1, so the weight becomes 4.84/3 ≈ 1.61.

The Frakinator's full form also multiplies in a constant bear factor — (1.2/1000) × √5000 × √Ntot — plus your troops' base attack (higher tier = higher base = more damage), and hero skills of the lead and of joining heroes add multiplicative boosts on top. None of that changes the √ and split rules on this page.

Fill the rally

A rally holds 1 lead + 14 joiners. Its troop capacity is split evenly: capacity ÷ players = your fair share.

A 900k rally with 15 players → 60k each.

FULL RALLY — 15 players ≈ 3.9×

All 15 slots filled beats a few fat ones. Always. Because rally damage is every player's √ added up — more players at fair share = more damage from the same 900k.

And you can't carry the rally yourself anyway: your march is capped (~100–150k on our server), so stuffing one rally just wastes its slots.

TRY IT — FILL THE RALLY

Fair share: 60,000 per player · rally hits 3.9× harder than one player carrying it all.

Why, in one line of maths

15 players × √(900k ÷ 15) = √(15 × 900k) ≈ 3.9× one player's √900k. Fewer players → fewer sqrts → less damage. There's no way around it.

Spread your marches

You can field 5–6 marches. Put them in 5–6 different rallies. That's rule one and two doing their job at the same time.

TRY IT — YOUR TROOPS, YOUR MARCHES

66,667 per march · 2.4× the damage of one big march. Same troops, nothing spent.

400k in one march isn't even an option on most servers (march cap ~100–150k). Spreading across all your marches is the only way to use what you've got. Under ~350k total, equalised marches are best — the ratio barely matters until you're past the line (troop mix).

Why 2.4×, in one line of maths

Your pool P across Q marches: 1 march scores √P, but Q marches score √(Q × P). Six marches = √6 ≈ 2.4×. Same troops, no extra spend.

Keep moving

A rally fires 5 minutes after launch. Parked troops score nothing — and the event only runs 30 minutes.

Why stagger? A rally fires exactly 5:00 after launch — that window is fixed. Staggered launches mean there's always a rally about to fire, so joiners never sit parked waiting out a full countdown.

Staggered waves = a fire roughly every minute, not one big blob:

W1
0:00 → 5:00
W2
1:00 → 6:00
W3
2:00 → 7:00
W4
3:00 → 8:00
0:002:004:006:008:00

The ideal, after the first wave: don't join a rally with more than a minute to launch. Join late, fire, go again. The reality: you join the rallies you can — the good leads launch when they launch, and only cities close enough to the bear can reach the slots. And marching is the real clock: out to the rally, to the bear, back home eats most of a joiner's march time. You're optimising marching, really.

The timing, in detail
  • Fire = launch + 5:00, always.
  • Event length: 30:00.
  • Wave groups launch at 0:00, 1:00, 2:00, 3:00 → fires at 5:00–8:00.
  • No 4:00 group: a lead starting that late can't realistically fit in five rallies — marching to and from the bear costs ~30s per rally at least, so a 3:00 launch is already cutting it close. The 4:00 slot still suits a newer or weaker lead: low pressure, no need to rotate fast.
  • Join late: after the first wave, aim for rallies with ≤1:00 to launch, not fresh ones.
  • After firing, troops fight + travel home (~30s) before the next launch.
  • A march parked in a rally that already fired is missing the next one.

Troop mix

Archers hit hardest per troop. And the mix only starts to matter once you're past ~350k troops — under that, equalised marches are best and the ratio barely matters.

Lean archers

Archers weigh ≈ 1.47 per troop at T6 — (4/3) × 1.1, where the 1.1 is their +10% vs infantry and the bear is all infantry. Cavalry 1.0, infantry 0.33. Same leader, same march — archers simply hit harder.

10 / 10 / 80

Past ~350k, run 10% infantry, 10% cavalry, 80% archers in every march, and leave the weaker infantry and cavalry at home. It's not the perfect ratio for any one lead — but you'll join rallies from a dozen different leads, and perfect per lead is a fool's errand. 10/10/80 is close enough everywhere, and you can actually do it.

~5k or skip it

Under ~5,000 troops of a type, the sqrt barely registers. In a 10/10/80 march at fair share, the 10%s land ~6.5k — right around that floor. Any smaller, fold it into the archers.

The perfect bear event isn't humanly possible. Do the heuristics.

When ratios start to matter

A full rally pays ~65k per player — a 1M rally (Town Center 30, our server) split across 15 slots. Times your 5–6 march slots, plus one more if you're leading a rally yourself (leading doesn't use a normal queue) — and you reach about 350k troops. Under that, everything you own fits in your marches: equalised marches are best, ratio be damned. Past it, some troops stay home, so what you send decides your damage — and that's when archer-heavy starts to win.

If you like the maths

Maximising Σ baset·At·√nt over the split (finf + fcav + farc = 1) is a Lagrange-multiplier problem; the solution is ft ∝ (baset·At)² — the weights (Ainf/3, Acav, 4.4·Aarc/3), squared. The bear factor L cancels out of the ratio, so the mix depends only on the lead's attack factors. That "preferred mix" a lead asks for is literally this — and 10/10/80 is the doable average of all those per-lead optima.

Tier & heroes — the fine print
  • Troop tier: damage scales with your troops' base attack — send your highest tier. The weights above are T6-era numbers.
  • T7+ / TG3+: archers gain another ×1.1 vs the bear (all-infantry), weight 4.4/3 → 4.84/3 ≈ 1.61.
  • Heroes: the lead's hero skills multiply the whole rally's damage; joining heroes' skills multiply their own march. Reward brackets sit on this absolute scale.

The heroes

The lead's kit and the joiners' first skills are the last big multiplier left. Two jobs, two lists — and a hero that shines at one is usually dead weight at the other.

How to read this: a joiner contributes only their first skill to the rally — that one slot is the whole job. A leader brings their full kit and their stats. Same skill codes stack additively; different codes multiply. And defence does nothing to a bear.

Your reward bracket follows your own damage — not the rally's. Thresholds keep doubling, one extra Forgehammer per bracket, all the way to 38.4B+:

47M pays 8 hammers, 38.4B pays 18 — one extra per bracket. More players crossing brackets = more Forgehammers for the whole alliance. Fair-share rallies grow everyone — one whale hoarding rallies grows one player.

The cap

Alliances cap how many troops a march participant can send — usually right around fair share. It's a fairness rule, and the maths says it's nearly free: only players past ~364k archers feel it at all.

The maths of the cap

The line is ~364k archers — 65k fair share per march × 80% archers × 7 marches, because a rally lead with six queues fields all seven. Past it, the cap leaves some archers at home. It's a real nerf, but a slight one: every extra troop past your first hits softer (that's the √ again).

And it almost never costs a bracket: the thresholds keep doubling, so 47M → 90M is a 91% gap, and a few percent lost to the cap won't cross it — provided the alliance launches enough rallies that strong players can keep every queue full.

So cap away — except one day a month. After KvK, hold a God bear: the strongest players from both hunt time slots in one massive hunt, everyone within ~30s travel of the bear, rallies chained back-to-back for super-low dwell times — and the cap off: everyone full-sends, and the whales finally get to use every last archer.
