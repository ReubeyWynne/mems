1 The one-liner

Remember one sentence and you basically know the whole event:

The lead's stats × your troops — with a square root on your count.

THE LEAD Sets the power

Everyone in the rally borrows the lead's stats. Same lead, same power, for all 15 of you. Your own attack stats don't matter.

YOU Make your damage

Each player's march is scored on its own, using the square root of its troop count. Your march. Your sqrt. Your damage.

That's it. Everything below is just what those two facts mean in practice.

2 The square root

The sqrt is the whole game. Here's the only number you need: double your march → +41%, not +100%.

Same troops. Just split across more marches:

1.0×
2 marches

6 marches
2.4×
  • 2× the troops = +41%, not double
  • 4× the troops = 2.0×
  • 16× the troops = only 4.0×

First troops hit hardest. Every extra troop hits a little softer than the last. So bigger marches help a little — more marches help a lot.

The exact maths

Per player, per rally, damage is added over your three troop types:

Σ  baset × At(lead) × √(nt)

  • nt — troops of type t in your march.
  • At(lead) = (1 + atk%) × (1 + lethality%) of the lead, per type. Your own attack & lethality never enter it.
  • baset — the relative weights at T6: infantry ⅓, cavalry 1, archers 4.4/3 ≈ 1.47. The archer weight is (4/3) × 1.1 — the bear is all infantry and archers do +10% vs infantry. At T7+/TG3+ archers pick up another 1.1, so the weight becomes 4.84/3 ≈ 1.61.

The Frakinator's full form also multiplies in a constant bear factor — (1.2/1000) × √5000 × √Ntot — plus your troops' base attack (higher tier = higher base = more damage), and hero skills of the lead and of joining heroes add multiplicative boosts on top. None of that changes the √ and split rules on this page.

3 Fill the rally

A rally holds 1 lead + 14 joiners. Its troop capacity is split evenly: capacity ÷ players = your fair share.

A 900k rally with 15 players → 60k each.

FULL RALLY — 15 players ≈ 3.9×

All 15 slots filled beats a few fat ones. Always. Because rally damage is every player's √ added up — more players at fair share = more damage from the same 900k.

And you can't carry the rally yourself anyway: your march is capped (~100–150k on our server), so stuffing one rally just wastes its slots.

TRY IT — FILL THE RALLY

Fair share: 60,000 per player · rally hits 3.9× harder than one player carrying it all.

Why, in one line of maths

15 players × √(900k ÷ 15) = √(15 × 900k) ≈ 3.9× one player's √900k. Fewer players → fewer sqrts → less damage. There's no way around it.

4 Spread your marches

You can field 5–6 marches. Put them in 5–6 different rallies. That's rule one and two doing their job at the same time.

TRY IT — YOUR TROOPS, YOUR MARCHES

66,667 per march · 2.4× the damage of one big march. Same troops, nothing spent.

400k in one march isn't even an option on most servers (march cap ~100–150k). Spreading across all your marches is the only way to use what you've got.

Why 2.4×, in one line of maths

Your pool P across Q marches: 1 march scores √P, but Q marches score √(Q × P). Six marches = √6 ≈ 2.4×. Same troops, no extra spend.

5 Keep moving

A rally fires 5 minutes after launch. Parked troops score nothing — and the event only runs 30 minutes.

Staggered waves = a fire roughly every minute, not one big blob:

The timing, in detail
  • Fire = launch + 5:00, always.
  • Event length: 30:00.
  • Wave groups launch at 0:00, 1:00, 2:00, 3:00 → fires at 5:00–8:00.
  • After firing, troops fight + travel home (~30s) before the next launch.
  • A march parked in a rally that already fired is missing the next one.

6 Troop mix

Archers hit hardest per troop. Match the lead's mix, lean archers, and skip any type you can't send ~5k of.

Lean archers

Archers weigh ≈ 1.47 per troop at T6 — (4/3) × 1.1, where the 1.1 is their +10% vs infantry and the bear is all infantry. Cavalry 1.0, infantry 0.33. Same leader, same march — archers simply hit harder.

~5k or skip it

Under ~5,000 troops of a type, the sqrt barely registers. Top up the types the lead's ratio wants instead.

Match the lead

The lead's preferred mix is the one that squeezes the most out of their stats. Send what they ask for.

If you like the maths

Maximising Σ baset·At·√nt over the split (finf + fcav + farc = 1) is a Lagrange-multiplier problem; the solution is ft ∝ (baset·At)² — the weights (Ainf/3, Acav, 4.4·Aarc/3), squared. The bear factor L cancels out of the ratio, so the mix depends only on the lead's attack factors. That "preferred mix" a lead asks for is literally this.

Tier & heroes — the fine print
  • Troop tier: damage scales with your troops' base attack — send your highest tier. The weights above are T6-era numbers.
  • T7+ / TG3+: archers gain another ×1.1 vs the bear (all-infantry), weight 4.4/3 → 4.84/3 ≈ 1.61.
  • Heroes: the lead's hero skills multiply the whole rally's damage; joining heroes' skills multiply their own march. Reward brackets sit on this absolute scale.
